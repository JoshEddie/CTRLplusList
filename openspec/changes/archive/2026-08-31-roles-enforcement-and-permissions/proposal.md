## Why

Source issue: [Roles enforcement + Permissions section #194](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/194), a chunk of [MAP: Dependents and shared list management #181](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/181).

The `manager` role exists in the database and passes every gate, but nothing narrows it. `profile_members.role` admits `self | owner | manager` (`profiles-data-model` :59), and the shared write gate `authedWriter` — the one `server-endpoint-authorization` :330 requires every profile-scoped write to pass — admits all three alike. A manager acting as a profile can therefore delete its lists, delete its items, change their visibility, and master-unclaim a purchase. The role is a label with no teeth.

Where the line falls was settled by this change's design session (2026-08-30), and it **revises** [#182](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/182)'s "content-lite: add/edit items + ordering only" to something materially wider: **owners run the profile — its membership and its identity — and hold everything irreversible; managers do everything else about content.** A manager therefore creates and edits lists and items, orders them, and archives; an owner alone deletes, sets visibility, master-unclaims, edits the profile's identity, and administers membership.

The specs already name this change as the one that fixes it. `profiles-surface` :302 states outright that owner-only profile editing "is the floor it starts from" and that "which further rights a `manager` holds is settled by the capability that introduces profile permissions". `list-visibility` :57 authorizes a visibility change by comparing the list's owning profile against the acting profile alone — a comparison a manager passes. `claim-attribution` :152 derives master unclaim from item ownership with no role floor, and [#202](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/202) explicitly left that narrowing here.

Second, membership has no administration surface. Rows are created only by the phase-1 backfill and the dev seed; there is no way to admit a manager, remove one, or change a role. [#183](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/183) settled that surface as a Permissions section in the profile's space with a rail holding the profile at ≥1 owner, and both stand. What this change **revises** is #183's admission mechanism — direct add from the acting owner's mutual follows, with no accept step. Nothing in the app notifies an account that it has been made a manager and nothing asks it to agree, so a direct add conscripts someone into running another person's profile without their consent and without their knowledge. Admission is therefore by **single-use invite link**: an owner mints one, sends it however they like, and the recipient following it is the acceptance. The link is the accept step, which is why no separate notification system is owed here.

Also inherited: the `active-profile` spec's `profile_members.last_active_at` (write- and switch-triggered, hour-coarsened) landed with [#193](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/193), so a member row can render last-active with its plain meaning. Read-only viewing does not stamp it, and this change rules deliberately that it will stay that way — the section consults the column to spot a dormant manager, and dormant means *has done nothing*, not *has not looked*. And the `active-profile` doctrine that the active profile *is* the authorization context stands unchanged — role narrows what a write may do **after** the acting-profile equality check, never widens what it may reach.

## What Changes

- **The shared write gate becomes role-aware.** `authedWriter` (`lib/data/profile.gate.ts`) gains a required floor argument with exactly two values — `member` (`self | owner | manager`) and `owner` (`self | owner`) — and no default, so each profile-scoped write declares the floor it needs. One gate still, so a write added later cannot omit the check — but it must now choose a floor.
- **The floor applies to the profile a write is addressed to, which is not always the acting one.** Content writes are addressed to the profile the request acts as and pass the gate. Writes over the profile *itself* — its identity and its membership — are addressed to the profile the request names, because the Permissions section and the Settings form are reached at `/altvatar/[id]` without switching to the profile they administer. Those check the same `owner` floor against the actor's membership on the named profile, which is exactly how `profiles-surface` already authorizes the profile-update action today. Conflating the two would have made an owner's own controls refuse unless they happened to be acting as that profile.
- **The owner/manager power matrix is enforced server-side.** The `member` floor admits `createList`, `updateList`, `createItem`, `updateItem`, `updateItemStores`, `archiveItem`, `setListItems`, `removeListItem`, `updateItemLists` and `updatePriority`. The `owner` floor admits `setListVisibility`, `deleteList`, `deleteItem`, master unclaim, profile settings and identity, and membership administration. The boundary cases the matrix rules on explicitly: archive is a manager act (reversible, deletes nothing, and the item stays on every list); removing an item from a list is a manager act (the two bulk paths, `setListItems` and `updateItemLists`, cannot be split by direction anyway); list creation *is* within a manager's reach, and `deleteItem` carries no authorship carve-out because [#229](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/229) settled `updated_by_user_id` as last-mutator and refused a creator column, so one is unimplementable.
- **`setListVisibility` goes to owners, and that call is flagged for revisit.** Reach is an ownership act even though the thing it hangs off is content: a manager flipping a managed profile's list Hidden → Shared exposes it to followers and the feed, and while the flag flips back, the exposure does not. [Recorded on #335](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/335#issuecomment-5470080412), whose publication axis — if it lands — would split the single knob into reach (arguably a manager's) and publish (an owner's), a better split than the one available today.
- **A Permissions section in the profile's space.** Lists the profile's members with role, avatar, and last-active; owners administer from it. It renders for **managed profiles only** — a managed profile has no `self` row by construction ([#182](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/182), revised 2026-08-18), and a self-profile is not administrable, so the section's population is uniformly `owner`/`manager` and no rule is owed for a `self` row.
- **Admission is a single-use invite link, and it is the only route in.** An owner mints a link carrying the profile and the role it grants — `owner` or `manager`, chosen at mint time and defaulting to `manager` — and sends it to anyone, related to them or not. Redeeming is an explicit act by a signed-in recipient and never the page load, because a link unfurler or a prefetch would otherwise spend a single-use link before the human opened it. A link dies on first redemption or seven days after minting, whichever comes first, so an unsent link left in a chat log is not a standing grant. Consuming the link and writing the membership row go in **one data-modifying CTE**: a spent link with no membership behind it is unfixable, since the recipient cannot redeem again and the owner cannot see that it happened. An unknown token, an expired one and an already-spent one refuse identically, so a stranger holding a guessed token learns nothing about whether it ever existed. Redeeming as an account that already holds a membership consumes the link and leaves the existing role untouched — a link admits, it never promotes or demotes. Redemption is refused where a block edge stands in either direction between the recipient and the owner who minted the link: it is the one exclusion the retired mutual-follow pool carried that admission still owes.
- **An outstanding invite is a row in the roster.** A minted link is not handed back in the minting overlay and then forgotten: it takes a seat in the Permissions section beside the memberships, carrying the link to copy, the role it grants, and the withdrawal of both — and it is replaced by the real member row when somebody redeems it. The row is an owner's alone, because it carries the token and a token is the grant itself: rendering one to a manager would let them admit a member with a link they cannot mint.
- **Membership administration actions**: issue an invite link, revoke or re-role an outstanding one, remove a member, and change a member's role. Any member may remove *themselves*; owners may remove others. Demotion is only ever done to another member — self-demotion is not offered. Every administrative action is owner-gated and re-verified server-side against the actor's membership on the named profile.
- **A ≥1-owner floor, owed on owner removal alone.** With no interactive transactions available this is a single-statement guarded write (`DELETE … AND EXISTS(another owner)`), not a read-then-write and not an index — no unique index can express "at least one". Demotion needs no such guard: with self-demotion off the table the demoting actor is necessarily a surviving owner, so a guard there would be the redundant kind CLAUDE.md forbids. The residual concurrent-race window is named and accepted, because it lands on an ownerless profile — a state [#187](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/187) already settled the app tolerates.
- **Master unclaim narrowed to owners**, and the `list-item-management` contradiction carried over from the `active-profile-switcher` spec-review (finding A6) reconciled: :385 says `removePurchase` resolves "the profile the request acts as" while its own scenario at :399 names the self-profile, and the code (`canRemovePurchase`, `lib/data/purchase.ts:175`) matches the scenario. The requirement prose is corrected to name which profile each leg of the three-way comparison takes. The claimer and purchaser legs are untouched — both compare the self-profile, so they answer *whose claim is this* rather than *what role do you hold*.
- **A manager sees forbidden affordances rendered disabled, not hidden** — so the space reads as restricted rather than as a surface with no such feature, with the server action as the real enforcement (never the disabled control). This **inverts** the treatment `profiles-surface` :252 fixed for the Settings form, which requires the submit control absent rather than disabled; that requirement is revised so one treatment governs the whole space.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `profile-permissions`: the role→power matrix (which of `self`/`owner`/`manager` passes which class of profile-scoped write), admission by single-use invite link, membership administration (remove, change role, self-removal), the ≥1-owner floor, and the Permissions section that surfaces all of it.

### Modified Capabilities

- `server-endpoint-authorization`: the shared profile-scoped write gate stops treating the three write roles alike — passing the gate becomes role-conditional on a two-valued floor, and each write declares its floor. A third exemption joins the two the requirement already carries: a write authorized by a single-use bearer capability, whose actor holds no membership on the profile by definition and so has no role for any floor to test.
- `profiles-surface`: the profile's space gains a Permissions section alongside Settings, and the manager treatment across the whole space becomes disabled-not-absent, revising the Settings form's existing rule.
- `list-visibility`: visibility change requires an `owner`-or-`self` role on the acting profile, not merely that the acting profile owns the list.
- `claim-attribution`: master unclaim requires an `owner`-or-`self` role on the item's owning profile, not membership alone.
- `list-item-management`: `removePurchase`'s authorization prose reconciled with its own scenario — naming self-profile vs active profile per comparison leg.
- `e2e-management-flows`: a manager-role flow covering both what a manager may do and what they are refused.

## Impact

- **Data layer**: `lib/data/profile.gate.ts` (role-aware gate), `lib/data/profile.ts` (member reads for the Permissions section), new membership-administration and invite actions — file-size bands make a separate `profile.members.*` module pair likely rather than growing `profile.actions.ts` (447 lines). Call sites that must declare a floor: 13 in all — `list.actions.ts` (4), `item.actions.ts` (4), `listItems.actions.ts` (3), and `item.associations.ts` (2 — `updateItemStores`, `updateItemLists`). `removePurchase` is **not** among them: guests reach it, so it takes `authedIdentity()` directly and never passes the gate — its owner floor lands inside `canRemovePurchase` (`lib/data/purchase.ts:175`) instead.
- **Schema**: one migration, adding a `profile_invites` table — the token as primary key, the profile it admits to, the role it grants, the account that minted it, minted-at, expires-at, and a nullable redeemed-at that is the single-use marker. On `profile_members` nothing changes: `role` and `last_active_at` both exist, and `ride_along` stays as it shipped, unread. The `(user_id, profile_id)` primary key makes a redemption by an account that already holds a membership idempotent via `ON CONFLICT`.
- **Caching**: the member list read consumes `profileMembers` plus `profilesOfUser(userId)` (`lib/cacheTags.ts`). Every membership write — add, remove, role change — must fire `profilesOfUser` for the affected member's account (their Profiles page and switcher both read it) as well as the coarse `profileMembers`; a removal must invalidate the removed account's tag, not only the acting owner's. A redemption is a membership write like any other and fires the same pair for the redeeming account. The Permissions section also lists the profile's outstanding invites, so minting, re-roling, revoking and redeeming each fire that read's own per-profile tag.
- **Revocation needs no new contract**: `active-profile` already requires membership re-verification on every resolution and specifies fallback to the self-profile, so a removed member acting as the profile self-heals with no error.
- **UI**: the Permissions section routes through existing primitives — `button-system`, `menu-system`, `confirm-dialog-system` for destructive removal, `form-shell-system` for the invite-minting overlay. No page-scoped one-off classes. The section names no route; `profiles-surface` keeps sole ownership of the space's address. The redemption page is a new route under `(main)`, so the onboarding layout short-circuit carries a brand-new account through the gate and back to the invite it was sent, with the URL untouched.
- **Tests**: one seeded seat of new fixture work. `scripts/seed-dev-users.ts` already makes the viewer `owner` on `dev-profile-owned` (with a manager to act on) and `manager` on `dev-profile-managed`, and `dev-profile-owned` carries exactly one owner, so the ≥1-owner refusal is reachable from the existing fixtures. The manager e2e flow needs one more: `dev-profile-workshop`, a seat reserved for a spec that writes lists and items it cannot clean up, because `dev-profile-managed` is simultaneously the never-acted-as ordering fixture and the empty-lists fixture, and a single write as it destroys both.
- **[#298](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/298) loses a seed it expected**: it names the mutual-follow add pool by this issue number as an approximation of association to build from. That pool is no longer built, so the association remap starts from the follow graph itself rather than from an approximation this change would have left behind. Nothing throwaway ships here.
- **Not in scope**: the ride-along toggle and any reader for `profile_members.ride_along` — cut 2026-08-30 (owner). The control cannot be labelled in terms a viewer can act on, and the concept belongs to the association model #298 overhauls; the column stays unread, exactly as it shipped, and no follower-reach behaviour changes. Also out: delivering the invite — the owner copies the link and sends it by whatever channel they like, so no email path and no notification ships; a read-path `last_active_at` stamp (ruled out above); follower/approval surfaces (carried to the circle remap, #298).
- **Absorbed, not deferred: the `/profiles` → `/altvatar` route rename.** An earlier draft listed the `issue-333` route drift in `profiles-surface` as out of scope and [#304](https://github.com/JoshEddie/CTRLplusList/issues/304)'s to sweep. This change's own delta amends `profiles-surface` and `menu-system`, so leaving their route text naming a 404 was not an option. The rename is carried in canonical `openspec/specs/profiles-surface/spec.md` and `openspec/specs/menu-system/spec.md`, and no `/profiles` reference remains in `openspec/specs/`, `app/`, `lib/` or `e2e/`. #304 is unaffected — its scope is id-kind naming and module placement, not routes.
